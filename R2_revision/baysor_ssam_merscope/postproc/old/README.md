# Archive — pre-2026-08-20 state of the A1 post-processing scripts

Snapshots of `A1_figures.R`, `A1_filter_de.ipynb` and `A1_granule_subtypes.ipynb` as they stood
before the WT-vs-AD differential-expression arm was removed. Kept because `postproc/` had never
been committed at the time, so this is the only copy of that work.

## What was removed from `A1_filter_de.ipynb`

Cells 16–24: the per-detection and 50 µm pseudo-spot WT-vs-AD Wilcoxon DE, the matched mcDETECT
reference DE, the `gseapy.prerank` GSEA, and the pathway-reference check. Together they produced
`DE_*_AD_vs_WT.csv`, `GSEA_*_AD_vs_WT.csv`, `pathway_reference_check_all.csv` and
`pathway_reference_check.jpeg` under `output/postproc/` (git-ignored; those files were left in
place, they are simply no longer regenerated).

## Why

The arm could not distinguish the detection methods, for reasons that are properties of the
contrast rather than fixable defects:

- The manuscript never ran a WT-vs-AD GSEA. Every published GSEA is `Subdomain 1_vs_Subdomain 2`,
  so the mcDETECT "reference" this check scored against was generated by the notebook itself and
  had never been a result.
- The reference is null. mcDETECT's own WT-vs-AD ranking has **0 of 69 terms at FDR < 0.05** at the
  detection level and 4 at the spot level; 16 of the 22 rankings have none at all. The comparison
  was null against null.
- The contrast does not need a detector. Unfiltered Baysor's per-gene AD-vs-WT logFC correlates
  **r = 0.96** with a logFC computed from raw transcript counts with no detection step at all
  (mcDETECT granules: r = 0.76). A contrast recoverable without detecting anything cannot
  adjudicate detection quality.
- The check ran backwards. Unfiltered Baysor and SSAM placed the manuscript's subdomain pathways
  *nearer the top* (median percentile 0.072) than mcDETECT did (0.087), and only 15% of those
  reference pathways were even matchable, because the Python arm queried Enrichr
  `GO_Biological_Process_2023` with uppercased mouse symbols while the manuscript uses `msigdbr`
  mouse C5:BP.

Two further limits applied regardless of method: the panel is 290 genes, which leaves only 69
testable GO BP terms whose leading edges overlap heavily; and there is one biological sample per
condition, so the DE treated up to 3.5 M detections as replicates.

## Replaced by

Three analyses on axes where detection quality does matter — per-region detection density,
compartment-marker entropy, and DE/GSEA anchored on mcDETECT's own neuropil subdomains. See the
parent `README.md`.

`A1_granule_subtypes.ipynb` is archived for reference only; nothing was removed from it. It is
also the ancestor of the live `postproc/A1_ssam_subtypes.ipynb` — see the correction below.

Replicated mcDETECT's granule-level downstream chain on the Baysor/SSAM detections: subtyping →
subtype density → neuropil microdomains → microdomain DE, for the `markers` setting only. It was
run only as far as its cluster×marker heatmap (`baysor/markers/pop2`) and never wrote a result to
disk.

## Why it was retired

Every question it was built to answer is now answered elsewhere, better:

- **Subtype density** was blocked here by `MANUAL_SUBTYPE_MAPPING`, a per-method manual call that
  was never filled in. It was first replaced by an overall detection-density analysis in
  `A1_filter_de.ipynb`, and that was itself retired the next day (see the section below). Subtype
  density now lives in `A1_ssam_subtypes.ipynb` §5, for SSAM only, where the manual mapping *is*
  answerable.
- **Microdomain DE** is superseded by `A1_filter_de.ipynb` §5 (§6 in the pre-shift numbering). That version inherits mcDETECT's
  published microdomains instead of recomputing them, so the spatial partition is identical across
  arms and only the transcripts differ. It also always yields a table, whereas this notebook cannot
  produce one when the subtypes come out mixed.
- **"The subtypes come out highly mixed"** — the observation this notebook existed to demonstrate —
  is now a measured quantity from `A1_filter_de.ipynb` §4 (§5 in the pre-shift numbering): median
  effective number of markers 5.7–9.1 for Baysor against mcDETECT's 1.9, and a compartment-pure
  fraction of 0.000–0.019 against mcDETECT's 0.33. That is a stronger statement than a heatmap, and
  it needs no manual mapping. (Current pipeline values, `entropy_summary.csv`; the range quoted here
  originally covered the `markers` arm only.)

## What it showed, before it was retired

AD/WT density ratio across the nine anatomical regions (< 1 = fewer detections in AD):

| | median AD/WT | regions declining | r vs mcDETECT (log₂ ratios) |
|---|---|---|---|
| mcDETECT granules (published) | 0.763 | 6/9 | — |
| Baysor, 290 genes, unfiltered | 1.008 | 4/9 | 0.36 |
| Baysor, 290 genes, out-of-nucleus | 0.977 | 5/9 | 0.33 |
| **Baysor, 290 genes, + marker-enriched** | **0.781** | **6/9** | **0.64** |
| Baysor, markers, unfiltered / out-of-nucleus | 0.882 / 0.922 | 6/9 | 0.60 / 0.68 |
| SSAM, every population | 1.28 – 1.47 | **0/9** | 0.52 – 0.70 |

The filtered Baysor arm reproduced mcDETECT's pattern closely — olfactory areas 0.569 vs 0.566,
fiber tracts 0.474 vs 0.495 — while thalamus and midbrain still flipped sign. SSAM never declined in
any region: its apparent AD increase is essentially the capture-efficiency correction (1/0.8187 =
1.22×), and uncorrected its ratio is ~1.05, i.e. flat, because its maxima count is insensitive to the
~33% difference in transcript abundance between the two sections (WT 103.0 M vs AD 68.6 M panel
transcripts).

This was the only analysis in which filtering partially recovered mcDETECT's result. It is not used
in the response letter.

# Retired 2026-08-21: `FINDINGS.md`

An intermediate summary between the pipeline outputs and the response letter. Retired because it was
a third place for numbers to drift: the pipeline outputs are now the single source of truth and
`plans/Response_R2_comments1-2_Baysor_SSAM.docx` is written directly from them.

Kept here in full because it records the reasoning behind several framing choices — why the
post-synaptic half of the pathway result does not discriminate between methods, why the subdomain
contrast survives its genotype imbalance, and why the immune GO terms are annotation artifacts. The
numbers in it predate the density removal; where they disagree with the current pipeline output,
the pipeline wins.
